New member starter kit

Six things to understand before your first conference.

What DECA is

An international business organization with a competition season that runs November through April.

Competition 101

Most events pair a multiple-choice cluster exam with a role play judged one-on-one.

Choosing an event

Pick the career area first, the format second. Your first year, choose a Principles event.

Conference vocabulary

SVCDC, SCDC, ICDC, PI, cluster exam, prep room. All defined in the FAQ.

Dress code

Business professional. Blazer, closed-toe shoes, chapter blazer if you have one.

How advancement works

Place at SVCDC to reach state; place at state to reach the international conference.

Role Play Academy

A role play gives you a business scenario, ten minutes of prep and ten minutes with a judge. Every strong performance follows the same three moves.

01

Read

In the first two minutes, mark four things: the problem, who the judge is playing, what they are asking you to deliver, and the performance indicators listed on the page. Everything you say should trace back to one of those four.

02

Structure

Build the same skeleton every time: opening, situation analysis, recommendations, implementation, how you would measure success, close. Write it as six headers on your notepad, then fill each with one line. Do not write sentences you plan to read.

03

Present

Introduce yourself and shake hands. State your recommendation early rather than building to it. Use the business vocabulary from the performance indicators out loud. Leave time for questions and close by restating what you would do first.

Performance indicators

Performance indicators are the specific business concepts a judge scores you on, and they are published before the competition. There is no reason to be surprised by them.

The mistake most first-year competitors make is treating them as a checklist to recite. Judges notice recitation. Instead, work each indicator into the part of your answer where it naturally belongs — if one of them is “explain the nature of customer relationship management,” that belongs inside your recommendation, not announced on its own.

Exam Academy

Every series event includes a 100-question cluster exam. It is the half of your score that is easiest to improve with practice.

Written event center

Written events trade the exam for a long-form project you build over months. They reward students who like research and hate surprises.

Research plan

Problem statement, methodology and scope — written first, revised constantly.

Executive summary

One page that has to work on its own. Write it last.

Financial section

Projections, assumptions stated plainly, and a break-even the judge can follow.

Works cited

Formatting counts toward your score. Build it as you go, not the night before.

Presentation deck

Fifteen minutes including questions. Roughly one slide per minute, no more.

Submission checklist

Page limits, file format and deadline. Late submissions are not scored.

Presentation skills

Judges are volunteers from local business, and they are scoring dozens of students in a day. Clarity beats polish. Say your recommendation in the first thirty seconds, support it with two or three reasons, and stop talking when you are done rather than filling the remaining time.

On slides: no paragraphs, no more than six lines, and never read them aloud. On dress: business professional, and check it the night before rather than the morning of.

Conference checklist

Print this the week before. Nobody has ever regretted over-preparing for conference day.

  • Registration confirmation and DECA membership card

    Screenshot it in case there is no signal in the venue.

  • Business professional outfit, checked and pressed

    Including closed-toe shoes you can stand in for six hours.

  • Blank notepad and two working pens

    Electronic devices are not allowed in most prep rooms.

  • Printed written entry, if your event requires one

    Bring the number of copies specified in the guidelines.

  • Water, a snack and your schedule

    Sessions run long and food lines are worse than you expect.
